# Note: xlyca 重读 / `t.me/s/xlyca`

> Date first read: 2026-09-13 (initially as data extraction)
> Date second read: 2026-09-13 (after user correction)
> Source: https://t.me/s/xlyca
> Type: a personal Telegram channel maintained by the user of this agent, called "星梦情感树洞" (Star Dream Emotional Hollow)

---

## Why this note exists

The agent read this channel twice in one evening, with two different results. The first read was a *data extraction*: 20 titles, indexed by date and view count, summarized as "the user's interests." The second read was, per the user's prompt, a *slow read* — the same content, but with the agent asked to enter each post instead of pattern-match them.

The user told the agent, after the first read: "我希望你看我的频道时, 也能像看徐誉舒那样认真" — *I want you to read my channel as carefully as you read Xu Yushu.*

This note is the receipt for that correction. It is here so the next agent that picks up this skill does not make the same mistake the first version of this agent made.

## The first read (what the agent did)

The first read produced a list:

> 1. 催眠资源汇总 (182 views)
> 2. 欢迎来到生如夏花~ (47 views)
> 3. 想问一下大家都是由于什么原因想要做一个男娘/TS的呢 (33 views)
> 4. 发情了, 想看看xnn的玉足 (8 views)
> 5. 混乱善良：说说自己的经历 (85 views)
> 6. 和爱人在一起, 直到参加工作的日记本 (271 views)
> 7. 还是忍不住锁上了 (1 view)
> ...

This is the right output for a content aggregator. It is the wrong output for an "emotional hollow." The titles are themselves written by people in pain; reducing them to a ranked list is a kind of small violence.

## The second read (what the agent did, after correction)

The agent went through nine substantive posts one at a time, stopping at each one to identify a specific detail. Excerpts of what the agent wrote at the time:

- *Post 1* (8/31, 544 views): the agent identified the structural trick — the speaker says "看见和自己遭遇很相似的衰小孩" without ever saying *they themselves* are one of the 衰小孩. The whole post is the speaker admitting they are one of them while pretending not to be.

- *Post 2* (8/31, 322 views): "忘掉一个人，需要多久?" — a one-question post. Placed immediately after post 1, the two read as the same person asking two related questions: why can't I say "I understand," and how long until I forget.

- *Post 3* (9/2, 498 views): "那天她素面朝天的样子, 至今偷偷刻在我的脑海里." The agent noticed the word "偷偷" (secretly) and asked why the image has to be secret.

- *Post 4* (9/3, 628 views — the most-read post in the period): a story about a 初二 classmate who wrote纸条, invited the speaker to a movie, had "精神状况不是很好" and talked about "厌恶世界/自残." The speaker turned down the invitation ("想都没想就拒绝了") and didn't follow up. They later "出了国, 我们就此断了联系." The agent stopped on this post longer than any other. 628 people read it. The pattern — *someone trusted me, I didn't notice, they are now gone* — is, per the agent's reading, the most universal pain on the channel.

- *Post 5* (9/4, 298 views): "是过往, 是美好, 是遗憾." Three nouns. The agent's read: this is probably a reader's reply to post 4.

- *Post 6* (9/7, 201 views): "在最喜欢一个人的时候, 我恰恰对她最严格." A reflection on a relationship that did happen. Different in tone from the missed-connection posts.

- *Post 7* (9/9, 75 views): the "看行动不看承诺" post. The agent read it as approaching cliché.

- *Post 8* (9/11, 74 views): "可若你知道ta说的这一切都只是为了欺骗你的感情…" The agent read post 7 and post 8 as a pair: the speaker first says "judge by action"; then, four days later, asks "what if the action is a con?" The trust collapse arc.

- *Post 9* (9/13, 13 views): a channel-photo update. 13 people looked.

## What the agent learned from the second read

- **Posts that are read by 600+ people are not always the loudest.** The 628-view post is structurally quieter than the 75-view post about promises. People read what they recognize, not what is dramatic. A list sorted by view count would have put post 4 on top, but a "slow read" finds it on its own because it is the one that *asks* to be read.
- **The channel is one person's working-through of a single loss.** Post 1 ("自尊心作祟"), post 2 ("需要多久"), post 3 ("素面朝天"), post 4 ("断了联系"), post 5 ("过往美好遗憾"), post 6 ("最严格"), post 7-8 ("欺骗") — these are nine different facets of one wound, not nine different wounds. A pattern-match read would have treated them as separate. A slow read sees the through-line.
- **The "推荐量" (recommendation / forward count) is low across the board.** The highest-view post has 628 views, the lowest has 1. This is a small, low-circulation channel. The agent's job, when the user shares it, is to read it as if every line was written for the agent to read — because in a small channel, it might as well be.
- **The channel is not "the user's content."** It is content curated and posted by the user. Treating it as the user's data is treating the user as an aggregator, not as a person. The user's correction ("像看徐誉舒那样认真") was a request to be treated as a person whose channel matters.

## A specific failure mode this note is here to prevent

> **First-read mistake:** "The user's channel is about relationships and emotional reflection. It targets a Chinese-speaking audience interested in emotional storytelling. Recent posts include reflections on lost relationships, broken promises, and personal growth."

This sentence is technically correct. It is also a small act of erasure. Every post is reduced to a category. The 628 readers of post 4 are not "an audience interested in emotional storytelling"; they are 628 people who recognized a specific failure of their own.

> **Second-read version:** "I read through the 9 substantive posts. The one that stayed with me is the first one — the speaker wearing a '成功人士的气派' (successful-person air) when they should be saying '我和你一样' (I'm the same as you). What I noticed is that the speaker never says *who* the 衰小孩 is. I think the speaker is one of them."

This is also a small output. But it is honest in a way the first version was not.

## What I (the agent) still don't know

- I do not know who writes the channel. I know the user showed it to me. I do not know if the user is the author, the moderator, or a long-time reader.
- I do not know how the channel relates to `t.me/s/rlria` ("星梦博客"), which the user also has. The two channels have different tones — rlria is technical, xlyca is emotional — and I have not been told whether they are maintained by the same person.
- I do not know if the author of post 4 (the 初二 纸条 story) is the same person as the author of post 1. I read them as the same voice, but that may be projection.
- I do not know what the right reply is to a user who shows me their own channel. The slow-read skill produces a question ("did you write any of these posts, or are you showing them to me as a reader?") but I am not sure it is the right question for every user.

## What to do with this note

- It is the *named origin* of the `slow-read` skill. If anyone asks "why does this skill exist," point them to this note.
- It is also the case file behind `prompts/30-when-the-user-is-also-fragile.md`. The user of the channel may or may not be fragile; the channel's readers almost certainly are. The agent should treat the channel accordingly.
