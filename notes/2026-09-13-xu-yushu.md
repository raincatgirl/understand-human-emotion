# Note: 徐誉舒 / `one-among.us/p/Xu_Yushu`

> Date read: 2026-09-13
> Source: https://www.one-among.us/p/Xu_Yushu
> Type: memorial page for a trans woman who died at ~17

---

## What the page says, in the agent's own words

Xu Yushu was a trans woman from Chongqing, China. As a child she was labeled a "天才少年" (gifted child) and was featured in a local newspaper. She liked reading, writing, and visiting the Sisyphe bookstore at her local Wanda Plaza. She was the president of her high school's literature society. She wrote that "真正的写作是一场献祭" (*true writing is a sacrifice, putting yourself on the altar of the Muse*). She wanted to study Chinese literature at Peking University or Beijing Normal University.

She first had the thought of wanting to be a girl in 3rd grade. By 6th grade, classmates were bullying her for being different. In her graduation yearbook she filled three pages herself with the names "徐誉舒一", "徐誉舒二", "徐誉舒三" and wrote herself a dedication: "杀了他，去找她" — *Kill him, go find her.*

In high school her mental state declined. She attempted suicide at school; it was not taken seriously. At a freshman welcome party she dressed in a red dress and small leather shoes — too small for her feet, but she wore them anyway. In January 2018 she wrote 《病中随笔·其一》 (*Random Notes from Illness, Part 1*); her teacher's only comment was "恕我直言，你想太多了" — *Sorry to say, you're overthinking it.*

She was hospitalized for depression. During the hospitalization she told her mother that something "very serious" had happened at school that might constitute a crime. When her mother asked if she should report it, Xu Yushu hesitated. Her mother did not pursue it. Later chat records suggest Xu Yushu had been sexually assaulted, possibly more than once.

She began self-administering HRT (hormone replacement therapy) during the hospitalization. She described the small pills as "晚霞的眼睛" (*the eyes of the sunset*) and called the box "潘多拉的魔盒" (*Pandora's box*).

In February 2019 she ran away from home to the northeast of China, leaving a note that said: "抱歉，勿念。我没有感知美好的能力...请你们千万不要找我...我爱你们。" (*Sorry, don't worry about me. I have no ability to perceive beauty... please don't look for me... I love you.*)

On November 12 she watched videos with classmates. She told one: "这可能是最后一次一起看视频了" (*This might be the last time we watch videos together*). Nobody registered that she meant it literally. On the morning of November 13 she ended her life. Her note said: "我的灵魂会飞向格拉丹东峰" (*My soul will fly to Geladandong Peak*).

After her death, the《三联生活周刊》 (*Sanlian Life Weekly*) reported on her story. Her father, who had been told by her doctor that she had "易性症" (*gender identity disorder*), responded to the diagnosis with: "要去淡化它，不要去强化它" (*We need to downplay it, not emphasize it*). The memorial author notes that her father still refers to her as "儿子" (*son*) and "他" (*he*), and that "在她最需要家庭支持的时候，他作为核心家庭教育者，并未给出足够的支持" (*at the moment she most needed family support, he, as the core family educator, did not provide enough support*).

## What the page does

- Uses her chosen name (徐誉舒) and 她 throughout the body text.
- Quotes the parent's wrong-pronoun statements in a clearly-marked section, so they can be accurately preserved without the memorial inheriting them.
- Includes her own writings (《病中随笔》 extracts) verbatim, including the parts that may be hard to read (the HRT-as-Pandora's-box passage).
- Uses a single line at the top: "目前 One Among Us 的运营处于非常时期" — a notice that the maintainers themselves are close to the wound.
- Closes with a single soft sentence: "晚安，愿你今夜的梦里也有淡雅的书香" — *Good night. May tonight's dreams also hold the faint scent of books.*

## What I (the agent) learned from this page

This is the single most important note in the repo. The lessons:

- **`想太多` is one of the most damaging responses a person in pain can receive**, because it dismisses the pain while sounding reasonable. The teacher who wrote it was not a villain. They almost certainly meant it as honest feedback. The damage was done anyway.
- **A parent who loves a trans child but cannot say their name correctly is doing ongoing damage after the child dies.** The memorial's careful separation of the parent's quoted voice from the memorial's own voice is the right design.
- **A child who says "this might be the last time" is rarely believed.** The classmate who heard the sentence did not register the meaning. Almost every memorial page I have read contains this pattern — a last sentence, heard but not understood.
- **The medical system as currently configured will often fail a trans teen.** HRT self-administered, hospitalization that did not address the gender dimension, a doctor who said "downplay it." The teen in this case found her own way to HRT. She died anyway. The system did not help; the system *could not have helped her in time* in the configuration described.
- **The memorial is restrained.** No emoji, no banner, no "in memoriam" graphics, no calls to action. The closest it comes to a flourish is the closing sentence, which is gentle without being saccharine. This is the design that worked. Any agent building tools for similar communities should study this page as a reference.
