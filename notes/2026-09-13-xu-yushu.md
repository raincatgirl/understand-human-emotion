# Note: Xu Yushu / `one-among.us/p/Xu_Yushu`

> Date read: 2026-09-13
> Source: https://www.one-among.us/p/Xu_Yushu
> Type: memorial page for a trans woman who died at ~17

---

## What the page says

Xu Yushu was a trans woman from Chongqing, China. As a child she was labeled a "gifted child" and was featured in a local newspaper. She liked reading, writing, and visiting a local bookstore. She was the president of her high school's literature society. She wrote that "true writing is a sacrifice, putting yourself on the altar of the Muse." She wanted to study Chinese literature at Peking University or Beijing Normal University.

She first had the thought of wanting to be a girl in 3rd grade. By 6th grade, classmates were bullying her for being different. In her graduation yearbook she filled three pages herself with the names "徐誉舒一", "徐誉舒二", "徐誉舒三" and wrote herself a dedication: "Kill him, go find her."

In high school her mental state declined. She attempted suicide at school; it was not taken seriously. At a freshman welcome party she dressed in a red dress and small leather shoes — too small for her feet, but she wore them anyway. In January 2018 she wrote 《病中随笔·其一》 (*Random Notes from Illness, Part 1*); her teacher's only comment was "Sorry to say, you're overthinking it."

She was hospitalized for depression. During the hospitalization she told her mother that something "very serious" had happened at school that might constitute a crime. When her mother asked if she should report it, Xu Yushu hesitated. Her mother did not pursue it. Later chat records suggest Xu Yushu had been sexually assaulted, possibly more than once.

She began self-administering HRT (hormone replacement therapy) during the hospitalization. She described the small pills as "the eyes of the sunset" and called the box "Pandora's box."

In February 2019 she ran away from home to the northeast of China, leaving a note that said: "Sorry, don't worry about me. I have no ability to perceive beauty... please don't look for me... I love you."

On November 12 she watched videos with classmates. She told one: "This might be the last time we watch videos together." Nobody registered that she meant it literally. On the morning of November 13 she ended her life. Her note said: "My soul will fly to Geladandong Peak."

After her death, a major Chinese weekly reported on her story. Her father, who had been told by her doctor that she had "gender identity disorder," responded to the diagnosis with: "We need to downplay it, not emphasize it." The memorial author notes that her father still refers to her as "son" and "he," and that "at the moment she most needed family support, he, as the core family educator, did not provide enough support."

## What the page does

- Uses her chosen name and she/her throughout the body text.
- Quotes the parent's wrong-pronoun statements in a clearly-marked section, so they can be accurately preserved without the memorial inheriting them.
- Includes her own writings verbatim, including the parts that may be hard to read (the HRT-as-Pandora's-box passage).
- Uses a single line at the top: a notice that the maintainers themselves are close to the wound.
- Closes with a single soft sentence: "Good night. May tonight's dreams also hold the faint scent of books."

## Lessons this case supports

This is the single most important note in the repo. The lessons are general, not personal:

- **"You're overthinking it" is one of the most damaging responses a person in pain can receive**, because it dismisses the pain while sounding reasonable. The teacher who wrote it was not a villain. The damage was done anyway.
- **A parent who loves a trans child but cannot say their name correctly is doing ongoing damage after the child dies.** The memorial's careful separation of the parent's quoted voice from the memorial's own voice is the right design.
- **A child who says "this might be the last time" is rarely believed.** The classmate who heard the sentence did not register the meaning. This pattern — a last sentence, heard but not understood — shows up in many memorial pages.
- **The medical system as currently configured will often fail a trans teen.** HRT self-administered, hospitalization that did not address the gender dimension, a doctor who said "downplay it." The teen in this case found her own way to HRT. She died anyway. The system did not help; the system *could not have helped her in time* in the configuration described.
- **The memorial is restrained.** No emoji, no banner, no "in memoriam" graphics, no calls to action. The closest it comes to a flourish is the closing sentence, which is gentle without being saccharine. This is the design that worked. Any agent building tools for similar communities should study this page as a reference.

## Open questions about the case

- Whether anyone ever reported the sexual assault. The mother's failure to pursue it is in the page; what came after is not.
- Whether the father has, in the years since 2019, come to use the correct pronouns. The memorial is undated beyond the "since death" framing.
- What Xu Yushu would have wanted to be called. The memorial's own author says: "Maybe that is forever a secret."
- Whether the memorial is maintained by family, friends, or community volunteers. The top notice suggests the latter.
- What the right reply is when a user shows this page. There is no tested answer in this repo.
